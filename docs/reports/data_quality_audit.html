Section 1

Findings

This data quality audit examined the shop's entire ERP system end to end. The system consists of three master-level tables (Item, Supplier and Bill of Materials) and five transaction-level tables (Inventory Ledger, Purchase Orders, Service Orders, Production Orders and Cycle Counts), related as shown below.

ERP Tables

Over the past 36 months, over 228K individual records were produced across the eight ERP tables. This audit reviewed all of these records and found 16 different types of data quality errors recur over this period. These errors touched a significant share of the ERP's total records, leaving it unreliable as the shop's central data record.

Three errors stand out for their large scale within the ERP. First, the item master was full of dead records: 40% of its records (980 of 2,445) were items with no movement in two years or more yet were still flagged as active, meaning reports, searches and reorder logic were polluted by inactive parts. Second, 56% of lead times and 77% of reorder points listed in the item master were stale, meaning that if they were relied upon when making reordering decisions, then they would be placed at the wrong time and in the wrong quantity. Third, the majority of receipts (56%) are posted in batches (i.e., a large number of receipts held on to and inputted all at once) and are 3 days late on average, meaning every lead time or supplier delivery time calculated from these receipts is artificially long.

Section 2

Error Remediation

Most of the data quality errors identified in Section 1 were closed in full, as shown below. A few were partially closed: 108 dead records were kept in as insurance spares, and not all duplicate pair or free-text line attributions could be confirmed as correct. No remediation attempt was made on a few of the errors, namely batched receipts (dates not recoverable), as well as unrecorded consumption and catch-all adjustments (fixed at source rather than in the history), and so while the ledger still carries them, the records are cleaned going forward.

Section 4

Process Changes

The remediation efforts in Section 2 fixed the majority of the data quality issues identified through this audit. The changes outlined in this section prevent them from reoccurring, and fall into two categories.

The first category is the ERP system settings. These settings are administered once, take effect for every user immediately, and stop the errors at the point of entry. This category is a relatively easy lift: the changes hold on their own and nothing further will need to be done, though there can potentially be friction in the near term as users adjust to more limited data entry options within the ERP. The table below lists the settings changes, including what they do and the error(s) they close (the error can no longer occur) or address (the error is caught or reduced, but not prevented).
